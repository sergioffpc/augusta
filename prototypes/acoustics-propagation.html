How a sound travels through a Map

With spec #802's model (M30, ADR-0010), what happens to a gunshot between the shooter and whoever hears it? How much it loses to distance and to the air, how much a wall lets through depending on its Surface material, when it is heard around a corner (pathing) and how the space it is fired in sounds (reflections). Click the map to place the shooter or yourself, use the buttons, and press Hear the shot.

What this prototype approximates that Steam Audio computes
  • The materials, the air absorption and the three bands are Steam Audio's presets, and the rule for what occludes, reflects and has a path is the spec's.
  • The geometry is 2D, seen from above. Reflections are image sources up to the 2nd order; Steam Audio ray-traces them.
  • Paths run through a grid of probes 2 m apart, linked where they see each other within 6 m.
  • The reverberation time is Sabine's, with a 3 m ceiling height; the yard has open sky above it.
  • It shows the behaviour, not the exact values Steam Audio would compute.

Questions the prototype raises

  • Door closed, path open. Paths are baked against the static Map and Props are left out (spec #802: "Props only occlude and transmit"). With the door closed, the baked path goes through the doorway and the sound arrives almost as if it were open. Steam Audio can validate paths at run time against the current scene, but the spec does not ask for it. See the walkthrough "The door is a Prop".
  • Direct and path at once. In line of sight the path coincides with the direct sound; playing both adds the same sound twice. Here the path plays only when the direct sound is occluded; the Engine has to decide the same.
  • Whose reflections? Here the reflections and the reverberation time are those of the Probe volume the shooter is in. When the shooter and the listener are in different spaces, which is heard? The spec says "a sound inside a Probe volume carries that space's reflections".
  • Concrete is almost deaf. Concrete's transmission presets (0.015 / 0.002 / 0.001) take about 36 dB off the lows and 54 dB off the mids: behind a concrete wall, what is heard is almost only the path around it.
