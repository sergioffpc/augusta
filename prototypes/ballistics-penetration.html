PROTOTYPE · THROWAWAY

What a round does when it strikes something

A rifle round leaves the muzzle at 800 m/s. At each surface it meets it either ricochets, when it strikes at an angle more grazing than the material's critical angle, or tries to go through, losing speed by v_exit² = v² − R · thickness / P, with the thickness measured by a query back from beyond the surface (ADR-0065, #820). Does that give the outcomes the spec asks for: through glass and a door, stopped by concrete, thickness and angle mattering, a ricochet you can learn, a round through one player into the next, less damage after cover? Drag on the map to aim, drag the shooter to move it.

What this prototype assumes that the spec does not fix
  • The world is seen from above, flat, at chest height: no gravity (a rifle round drops about 1 cm over these 20 m) and no head. Every collider is a box; a player is a torso circle (0.34 m across) and two arm circles (0.11 m across) 0.25 m to each side.
  • R is in m²/s² of speed lost per metre, so with P = 1 a material stops a muzzle round in v_muzzle² / R metres; the "stops a round in" column shows it. Every value in the catalogue below is a guess to tune, not a measurement.
  • The angle is measured from the surface: 90° is head-on, 0° grazing. Thickness along the path is the wall's thickness ÷ sin(angle), so an oblique shot meets more material.
  • The back query starts the maximum depth (1 m) beyond the entry and casts back to it; the exit is the first face it meets. If it starts inside a collider, no exit is found and the round stops in the surface it struck.
  • The bound is on strikes: a round passes through or ricochets off that many surfaces; the next one stops it where it touches. A player that is the next one is still hit.
  • A deviation is uniform within ±spread, one draw per penetration or ricochet, from a seed of the shooter's Entity ID and the Shot's tick. A ricochet that the deviation would turn back into the surface is reflected without it.
  • Hitboxes are judged where they stand: there is no Shooter's delay here.
