Hitting what you saw

A shooter sees the target where it was about 100 ms plus half a round trip ago, and its fire Command takes another half round trip to arrive. The Command reports its Seen time; the server takes the time from it to the tick it takes the Command in as the Shooter's delay, caps it at 250 ms, and tests every tick of the round's flight against the target's Hitbox as the Hitbox history held it that fixed delay ago (ADR-0044). Does a round aimed at what the shooter saw hit, at any ping, and how long has the victim already been behind cover, on its own screen, when it learns it was hit? Pick a walkthrough, or click the strip below to fire at that moment.

What this prototype assumes that the specs do not fix
  • Seen from above, flat, no gravity. The target's Hitbox is one 0.6 m circle; the walls are the Map, static, so judged where they are.
  • The tick rate is 60 Hz. The shooter's Seen time is the server time of its newest update (one-way latency behind) less the 100 ms Interpolation delay, as a steady ServerClock gives it; the shooter's aim is perfect, at the target as it saw it, or led by its seen velocity for the flight time.
  • The server takes the fire Command in at the first tick after it arrives plus the Commands queued before it, and fires the round on that tick from the shooter.
  • A victim's own screen runs ahead of the server by its own one-way latency plus its queue, since it predicts itself; it learns of a hit when the hit tick's update reaches it.
  • "First tick only" judges the first tick of the flight at the delay and the rest against the present; "none" judges everything against the present; "uncapped" is ADR-0044 without the 250 ms cap. A Hitbox history of 15 ticks cannot hold what "uncapped" asks for past 250 ms; here it is assumed to.
