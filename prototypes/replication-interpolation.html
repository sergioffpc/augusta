How other players reach your screen

Every tick the Authoritative server sends every client the same bodies, everyone's, plus what is that client's own (its acknowledged Command, its rifle, its health). A client never shows another player where the newest update put it: it keeps a short buffer of updates on the server's timeline, runs a clock that follows the newest one by at most 2% faster or slower than real time, and shows each player 100 ms (the Interpolation delay) behind that clock, between the two updates around it. That moment is the client's Seen time, which every Command reports for Lag compensation. Three clients, at different distances from the server, watch one player R. Do they see R smoothly, and how far in the past?

What this prototype assumes that the code does not fix
  • Seen from above, flat. R moves along a scripted path on the server at 60 Hz; nothing else moves.
  • Each client's frames run at a steady rate, apart from a hitch you add. Each frame does what OnInterpolation does today: advance the clock by the frame's time, then record the newest update that has arrived, if newer than the last recorded, and only that one; then sample 100 ms behind the clock.
  • Every message's latency is its link's one-way latency plus a uniform jitter; a message is lost on its own chance or inside a loss window; a latency spike adds to the latency for its length.
  • The server's clock and the real time are the same: tick k is sent at k / 60 s.
  • A stance shows as the body's size: standing, crouched, prone.
